A short history of British games — and where you fit in

Britain didn't just play games. It built the industry that makes them.

Forty years ago, a generation of teenagers taught themselves to code on kitchen-table computers and turned bedrooms into software houses. Today the games made in Britain are among the biggest entertainment products on Earth — and the industry still doesn't have enough people to make the next ones.

£8.76bn spent on games in the UK in 2025 — Ukie 73,000+ jobs in UK games — Ukie #1 games market in Europe — Ukie

Origins

How a home computer boom became a national industry

There was no games industry to join in 1982 — so people built one, in bedrooms, with cassette tapes and cover-taped magazine listings as their only teachers.

Early–mid 1980s

The bedroom years

The ZX Spectrum, BBC Micro and Commodore 64 put a programmable computer in ordinary British homes for the first time. Teenagers taught themselves BASIC and machine code, then sold what they made by mail order out of spare bedrooms and terraced houses.

  • 1983Matthew Smith, 17, codes Manic Miner in eight weeks in Wallasey — the first ZX Spectrum game with in-game music, and an overnight hit.
  • 1982–84Tim & Chris Stamper build Ultimate Play the Game into one of the decade's biggest studios with Jetpac, Sabre Wulf and Knight Lore.
  • 1984Cambridge undergraduates David Braben and Ian Bell fit an entire simulated galaxy into 32 kilobytes for Elite on the BBC Micro.
Late 1980s – 1990s

Going global

British studios stopped making games only for the home market and started making the games the entire world was playing.

  • 1991David Jones's DMA Design, in Dundee, ships Lemmings — a worldwide hit on almost every format that existed.
  • 1994The Stamper brothers' Rare, now Nintendo's key Western studio, releases Donkey Kong Country.
  • 1997DMA Design, relocated to Edinburgh, releases the first Grand Theft Auto. Rare follows with GoldenEye 007.
2000s

Built to last

The studios that survived the bedroom era became the backbone of the modern industry. DMA Design was renamed Rockstar North in 2002, David Braben's Frontier Developments (founded 1994) kept building, and a small Worms publisher called Team17 was quietly turning into something bigger.

2010s – now

The current wave

Crowdfunding, digital storefronts and mobile gave a new generation of British studios and solo developers a direct route to players. The games being made in Britain right now are some of the biggest entertainment products on the planet.

  • 2014Frontier Developments crowdfunds and ships Elite Dangerous, thirty years after the original Elite.
  • 2018Team17 lists on the London Stock Exchange.
  • 2026Grand Theft Auto VI, made by Rockstar North in Edinburgh, is due to launch on 19 November — one of the most anticipated entertainment releases ever made.

Now

Britain's game, right now

The UK is the largest video games market in Europe and one of the largest in the world — built by more than two thousand active studios and publishers, most of them nowhere near London.

People

Eight people who prove there's no one way in

Programmers, designers, writers and business leaders — real case studies, not job titles.

MS

Matthew Smith

Programmer · Software Projects

In 1983, seventeen-year-old Matthew Smith spent eight weeks coding Manic Miner in his home in Wallasey. Released for the ZX Spectrum, it was the first Spectrum game with in-game music and sold in huge numbers almost overnight.

Why it mattersYou don't need permission to start. You need a finished game.

T&C

Tim & Chris Stamper

Founders · Ultimate Play the Game → Rare

The Stamper brothers turned a small Spectrum outfit into one of the defining studios of the 1980s, then moved with the market — founding Rare and becoming Nintendo's key Western studio with Donkey Kong Country and GoldenEye 007.

Why it mattersThe best studios don't survive by staying still — they follow the hardware and the players.

DB

David Braben

Programmer, founder · Elite, Frontier Developments

As Cambridge undergraduates, David Braben and Ian Bell spent two years fitting a simulated galaxy into a BBC Micro for Elite (1984). Braben founded Frontier Developments in 1994 and is still its CEO, forty years on.

Why it mattersOne of the biggest technical achievements in games history came from a memory limit, not despite one.

DJ

David Jones

Founder, designer · DMA Design → Rockstar North

David Jones founded DMA Design in Dundee, made Lemmings a worldwide hit in 1991, then moved to Edinburgh and made the first Grand Theft Auto. His studio, renamed Rockstar North, went on to make GTA V.

Why it mattersScotland, not California, is where the biggest entertainment franchise on the planet is made.

DEB

Debbie Bestwick MBE

CEO, 1990–2024 · Team17

Debbie Bestwick co-founded Team17 in Wakefield in 1990, starting in sales and marketing rather than code. She became sole CEO in 2010, listed the company on the stock market in 2018, and grew it into a major indie publisher before stepping down in 2024.

Why it mattersGames need people who understand players and money, not only people who can write code.

SR

Siobhan Reddy

Studio director · Media Molecule

Siobhan Reddy moved from Australia to the UK at eighteen, worked her way through Perfect Entertainment and seven years at Criterion Games, then co-founded Media Molecule — directing LittleBigPlanet, Tearaway and Dreams.

Why it mattersBritain's games industry isn't only built by people born here — it's built by people who chose to come.

RP

Rhianna Pratchett

Narrative designer, writer

Trained as a journalist, Rhianna Pratchett moved into games writing in 2002. Her script for Heavenly Sword earned a BAFTA nomination, Overlord won a Writers' Guild award, and she was lead writer on the 2013 Tomb Raider reboot.

Why it mattersGames need the same craft as any other storytelling medium — a career here doesn't have to start with code.

DH

Demis Hassabis

Programmer, designer → Nobel laureate

At seventeen, Demis Hassabis won a magazine competition to work at Bullfrog Productions and ended up co-designing and lead-programming Theme Park (1994) with Peter Molyneux. He later founded Elixir Studios, then left games to study computer science and cognitive neuroscience before co-founding DeepMind. In 2024 he won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry for AlphaFold, DeepMind's system for predicting protein structures.

Why it mattersThe problem-solving you learn making games doesn't stay in games. It can end up solving problems in biology, medicine — anywhere.

Here

It's already happening in Northern Ireland

You don't have to move to Edinburgh or Guildford to start. It's happening closer than that.

Italic Pig — Holywood

An independent studio running for around eight years just outside Belfast, behind Schrödinger's Cat and the Raiders of the Lost Quark and the upcoming Paleo Pines.

Hypixel Studios — Derry~Londonderry

After Riot Games (League of Legends) acquired Hypixel Studios, maker of the hugely anticipated Hytale, it opened back-office and QA operations in Derry~Londonderry. In November 2025, Hypixel's original founders bought the studio and the game back, keeping it independent.

The Pixel Mill — Belfast

A co-working space for Northern Irish game-dev teams, run with Northern Ireland Screen — a physical front door into the local industry.
